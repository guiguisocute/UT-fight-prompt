***Minnie the Moocher*** is a 1932 cartoon starring [Betty
Boop](Betty_Boop "wikilink") and
[Bimbo](Bimbo_(Fleischer_Studios) "wikilink"), and featuring [Cab
Calloway](Cab_Calloway "wikilink") and his orchestra, singing the
[namesake hit song](Minnie_the_Moocher "wikilink").

Part of the [*Talkartoon*](Talkartoons "wikilink") series produced by
[Fleischer Studios](Fleischer_Studios "wikilink") and released by
[Paramount Pictures](Paramount_Pictures "wikilink"),[^1] it premiered in
[New York City](New_York_City "wikilink") on January 1, 1932[^2] at the
Rivoli Theatre (being shown alongside *Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde*), going
into general release on February 26 of that year.[^3]

The short is considered to be one of the most significant cartoons ever,
playing an important part on building up the popularity of Betty Boop,
as well as reinventing the [rotoscope](Rotoscoping "wikilink") process,
which had been invented by Fleischer almost two decades before, but had
fallen in disuse from the mid-1920s onwards. In 1994, *Minnie the
Moocher* was voted \#20 of the [50 Greatest
Cartoons](The_50_Greatest_Cartoons "wikilink") of all time by members of
the animation field.[^4]

## Plot

The cartoon opens with a [live action](live_action "wikilink") sequence
of [Cab Calloway](Cab_Calloway "wikilink") and his orchestra performing
an instrumental rendition of "[St. James
Infirmary](St._James_Infirmary_Blues "wikilink")".[^5]

[Betty Boop](Betty_Boop "wikilink") is scolded by her strict German
immigrant parents[^6] for not eating her
[hasenpfeffer](hasenpfeffer "wikilink") dish. While her father's head
turns into a [phonograph](phonograph "wikilink") as a result of his
blabbering, a flower tries to convince Betty to eat her dinner, only to
die after eating a bite. Betty then storms out sobbing (while her mother
changes [cylinders](Phonograph_cylinder "wikilink") on Papa's phonograph
head) and sings the [Harry Von Tilzer](Harry_Von_Tilzer "wikilink") song
"They Always Pick on Me" as she heads to her bedroom and decides to run
away from home.

Betty then writes a farewell note to her parents while the song "[Mean
to Me](Mean_to_Me_(1929_song) "wikilink")" plays in the background
([Koko the Clown](Koko_the_Clown "wikilink") makes a brief cameo popping
out of an inkwell, in a nod to the old *[Out of the
Inkwell](Out_of_the_Inkwell "wikilink")* cartoons) and phones her
boyfriend [Bimbo](Bimbo_(Fleischer_Studios) "wikilink"), who joins her.
Walking through the countryside as night falls, they both come across
some specters and venture in a cavern out of fright. Inside, they find a
[walrus](walrus "wikilink")-like apparition (voiced by and
[rotoscoped](rotoscoping "wikilink") to Calloway), who sings "[Minnie
the Moocher](Minnie_the_Moocher "wikilink")" with some haunted spirits.
Betty and Bimbo both change their minds about running away and rush back
home with the ghouls in hot pursuit as "[Tiger
Rag](Tiger_Rag "wikilink")" plays in the background. Betty makes it
safely back to her home and hides under the blankets of her bed. As she
shakes in terror, the note she earlier wrote to her parents tears,
leaving "Home Sweet Home" on it.

The film ends with Calloway and his band performing the instrumental
"Vine Street Blues".

## Reception

*[The Film Daily](The_Film_Daily "wikilink")*, on January 10, 1932,
wrote: "This Max Fleischer musical cartoon is one of the best turned out
so far with the cute pen-and-ink star, Betty Boop, who seems to be
getting more sexy and alluring each time, and her boyfriend, Bimbo. The
musical portion is supplied by Cab Calloway and his orchestra, and what
these boys can't do to the 'Minnie the Moocher' number isn't worth
mentioning. Cab and his boys are shown only for a brief moment at the
opening. Then a cartoon character, a big walrus with serpentine hips,
performs the gyrations to the tune of the 'Minnie' song. The effect is
short of a knockout, especially to those who are familiar with Cab's
stuff on the radio or stage or night club. Betty Boop's part in the
action concerns her running away from home because of her bad parents.
With Bimbo she goes into a cave, where spooky figures and eerie noise
give them such a scare that they beat it back home".[^8]
