6 takeaways

1.  01

    The team uses extensive external references from 1930s cartoons—such
    as Betty Boop or Popeye—to inform character proportions and
    stylistic choices without directly copying existing designs.

2.  02

    The design process for Cuphead bosses prioritizes simplicity and
    solid shapes, avoiding overly complex details that would be
    difficult to animate or rotate in 2D space.

3.  03

    To ensure technical feasibility, animators create rough blue-pencil
    sketches before committing to final designs, as the high-stakes
    nature of paper animation makes late-stage changes labor-intensive.

4.  04

    Complex animations, such as the tombstone boss slam, are often
    animated on 'twos' (one drawing every two frames) to manage the high
    volume of hand-drawn assets required for the game.

5.  05

    Studio MDHR maintains the aesthetic of 1930s animation by utilizing
    traditional paper-based animation, hand-inked frames, and watercolor
    backgrounds rather than digital simulations.

6.  06

    The game's '1930s cartoon' art style provides gameplay flexibility,
    allowing developers to implement surreal, non-sequitur boss
    transformations and mechanics that would not fit a more grounded
    visual theme.

## Summary

The development of the 2D action game Cuphead is defined by a commitment
to the aesthetic and production techniques of 1930s animation. The
primary objective of Studio MDHR was to create a game that felt
authentically rooted in the era of early cartoons like Betty Boop and
Popeye. To achieve this, the team utilized traditional paper-based
animation, hand-inked cels, and watercolor backgrounds, complemented by
a live-recorded jazz soundtrack. This approach prioritizes artistic
integrity and a distinct visual identity over the efficiency of modern
digital workflows.

The design philosophy centers on simplicity, utilizing solid, rounded
shapes and the "rubber hose" animation style, which allows for fluid,
exaggerated movement. When designing characters, the team emphasizes
gameplay potential and animation flexibility, often "frankensteining"
features from various 1930s references to ensure a cohesive look. The
animation process involves rigorous planning, including acting out
movements to establish key poses, followed by rough sketches to test
mechanics before committing to final, labor-intensive inking. By
avoiding rigid model sheets, the team maintains the loose, expressive
quality characteristic of early animation.

Beyond aesthetics, the 1930s style provides significant gameplay
advantages. Because the source material is inherently surreal and
imaginative, the developers are not constrained by realistic logic,
allowing for creative boss encounters that prioritize fun and variety.
By decoupling gameplay mechanics from narrative constraints, the team
can implement complex, unpredictable attack patterns that fit seamlessly
into the game’s whimsical world. Ultimately, the decision to employ
traditional, time-intensive methods serves both as a creative homage and
a functional tool to deliver a unique, high-quality player experience
that remains distinct within the modern indie game landscape.
