"**By a Waterfall**" is a 1933 song with music by [Sammy
Fain](Sammy_Fain "wikilink") and lyrics by [Irving
Kahal](Irving_Kahal "wikilink"). It was featured in an extravagant
choreographic arrangement in the film *[Footlight
Parade](Footlight_Parade "wikilink")* by [Busby
Berkeley](Busby_Berkeley "wikilink") that features his trademark human
[waterfall](waterfall "wikilink"), with vocal performances by [Dick
Powell](Dick_Powell "wikilink") and [Ruby
Keeler](Ruby_Keeler "wikilink"). It features a [water
ballet](synchronised_swimming "wikilink") of
[chorus](Chorus_line "wikilink") girls diving and swimming into the
water in elaborate geometric and floral patterns.[^1]

The lyrics of the song use the phrase "I'm calling you - oo-oo-oo" in
much the same way as the *[Indian Love
Call](Indian_Love_Call "wikilink")* from the operetta
*[Rose-Marie](Rose-Marie "wikilink")*.

Berkeley realized that screen choreography involved the placement and
movement of the camera as well as the dancers. Instead of filming
numbers from fixed angles, he set his cameras into motion on custom
built booms and monorails and if necessary, cut through the studio roof
to get the right shot.

Berkeley used a swimming pool that filled an entire soundstage. Its
walls and floor were glass, and before shooting started 100 chorus girls
took two weeks to practice their routines in it. The actual filming
lasted six days and required of water a minute to be pumped across the
set.[^2]

## Recordings

The recordings by [Guy Lombardo](Guy_Lombardo "wikilink") & His Royal
Canadians (vocal by [Carmen Lombardo](Carmen_Lombardo "wikilink")), [Leo
Reisman](Leo_Reisman "wikilink") & His Orchestra (vocal by Arthur
Wright) and by [Rudy Vallee](Rudy_Vallee "wikilink") are assessed by
[Joel Whitburn](Joel_Whitburn "wikilink") as the most popular in
1933.[^3]

### Other recordings

- [Chick Bullock](Chick_Bullock "wikilink")'s Levee Loungers recorded
  the song for Oriole Records (catalog No. 2780A) on October 30,
  1933.[^4]
- Dick Powell recorded the song for Brunswick Records (catalog No. 6667)
  on September 27, 1933.[^5]
- [Ozzie Nelson](Ozzie_Nelson "wikilink") and his Orchestra recorded the
  song for Vocalion Records (catalog No. 2547) on September 5, 1933.[^6]
