***The Skeleton Dance*** is a 1929 American animated [comedy
horror](comedy_horror "wikilink") short film directed and produced by
[Walt Disney](Walt_Disney "wikilink").[^1] It is the first installment
in the *[Silly Symphony](Silly_Symphony "wikilink")* series.[^2] In the
film,[^3] reanimated human [skeletons](skeleton "wikilink") dance and
make music around a spooky [graveyard](graveyard "wikilink")—a modern
film example of medieval European "[danse
macabre](Danse_Macabre "wikilink")" imagery. As a work published in
1929, it entered the [public domain](2025_in_public_domain "wikilink")
on January 1, 2025.[^4]

## Plot

![The full short](The_Skeleton_Dance_(1929).webm "The full short") The
short film begins with an owl perched on a branch, in front of the full
moon, then shows an empty graveyard with a church in the background. The
minute hand on the church's clock strikes twelve, causing its bell to
start tolling, which causes a group of bats to fly from the belfry. A
dog howls at the moon, while two cats fight over a grave. A skeleton
emerges from the grave and frolics, but at the sound of the owl, the
skeleton hides behind a grave. Upset about overreacting to the owl's
hooting, the skeleton detaches its head from its neck and chucks it at
the owl, knocking the owl's feathers off. Then the head bounces back to
the grave and returns to its body.

Next, four skeletons emerge from the grave and start dancing. One of
them takes two bones and plays its partner's spine and head to produce
music. Another skeleton dances alone and then plays a cat's tail as if
it were a violin. The crowing of a rooster tells them it's close to
dawn. The skeletons rush to hide, but their bodies collide and blend.
The skeletons, now mingled, rush to the grave and jump in. A pair of
skeleton feet get left outside. A skeletal arm emerges from the grave to
yank them in.

## Production

The origins of *The Skeleton Dance* can be traced to mid-1928, when Walt
Disney was on his way to [New York](New_York_City "wikilink") to arrange
a distribution deal for his new [*Mickey
Mouse*](Mickey_Mouse_(film_series) "wikilink") cartoons and to record
the soundtrack for his first sound cartoon, *[Steamboat
Willie](Steamboat_Willie "wikilink")*. During a stopover in [Kansas
City](Kansas_City,_Missouri "wikilink"), Disney paid a visit to his old
acquaintance [Carl Stalling](Carl_Stalling "wikilink"), then an organist
at the Isis Theatre, to compose scores for his first two Mickey shorts,
*[Plane Crazy](Plane_Crazy "wikilink")* and *[The Gallopin'
Gaucho](The_Gallopin'_Gaucho "wikilink")*. While there, Stalling
proposed to Disney a series of "musical novelty" cartoons combining
music and animation, which would become the genesis for the Silly
Symphony series, and pitched an idea about skeletons dancing in a
graveyard. Stalling would eventually join Disney's studio as a staff
composer.[^5] ![Iwerks was inspired by the frontispiece to Thomas
Rowlandson's 1816 edition of satirical etchings, *The English Dance of
Death.*](Rowlandson-english-dance-of-death.jpg "Iwerks was inspired by the frontispiece to Thomas Rowlandson's 1816 edition of satirical etchings, The English Dance of Death.")
Animation on *The Skeleton Dance* began in January 1929, with [Ub
Iwerks](Ub_Iwerks "wikilink") animating the majority of the film in
almost six weeks.[^6] Iwerks pulled inspiration for the skeletons from
"pictures drawn by the English cartoonist
[Rowlandson](Thomas_Rowlandson "wikilink")".[^7]

The soundtrack was recorded at [Pat
Powers](Pat_Powers_(producer) "wikilink")'
[Cinephone](Cinephone "wikilink") studio in New York in the following
month, along with that of the Mickey Mouse short *[The Opry
House](The_Opry_House "wikilink")*. The final negative cost
\$5,485.40.[^8]

## Reception

*[Variety](Variety_(magazine) "wikilink")* (July 17, 1929): "Title tells
the story, but not the number of laughs included in this sounded cartoon
short. The number is high. Peak is reached when one skeleton plays the
spine of another in [xylophone](xylophone "wikilink") fashion, using a
pair of thigh bones as hammers. Perfectly timed xylo accompaniment
completes the effect. The skeletons hoof and frolic. One throws his
skull at a hooting owl and knocks the latter's feathers off. Four Bones
brothers do a unison routine that's a howl. To set the finish, a rooster
crows at the dawn. The skeletons, through for the night, dive into a
nearby grave, pulling the lid down after them. Along comes a pair of
feet, somehow left behind. They kick on the slab and a bony arm reaches
out to pull them in. All takes place in a graveyard. Don't bring your
children."[^9]

*[The Film Daily](The_Film_Daily "wikilink")* (July 21, 1929): "Here is
one of the most novel cartoon subjects ever shown on a screen. Here we
have a bunch of skeletons knocking out the laughs on their bones, and
how. They do a xylophone number with one playing the tune on the other
spine. All takes place in a graveyard, and it is a howl from start to
finish, with an owl and a rooster brought in for atmosphere."[^10]

In 1994, *The Skeleton Dance* was voted \#18 of the [50 Greatest
Cartoons](The_50_Greatest_Cartoons "wikilink") of all time by members of
the animation field.[^11]

## Release

![The soundtrack was released on vinyl in
2016.](The_Skeleton_Dance_vinyl_release.jpg "The soundtrack was released on vinyl in 2016.")
To attract a national distributor for the *Silly Symphony* series, Walt
and Roy Disney arranged for *The Skeleton Dance* to run at the [Carthay
Circle Theatre](Carthay_Circle_Theatre "wikilink") in Los Angeles and at
the [Fox Theatre](Fox_Theatre_(San_Francisco) "wikilink") in San
Francisco in June 1929, while Pat Powers arranged for it to play at New
York's [Roxy Theatre](Roxy_Theatre_(New_York_City) "wikilink") from
July. In early August, [Columbia Pictures](Columbia_Pictures "wikilink")
agreed to distribute the Silly Symphonies, and *The Skeleton Dance*
played as a Columbia release in September at the Roxy, making it the
first picture in the theater's history to have a return engagement.[^12]

In March 1931, *[The New York Times](The_New_York_Times "wikilink")*
reported that the film had been banned in [Denmark](Denmark "wikilink")
for being "too macabre".[^13] The short's copyright was renewed in 1957,
which expired in 2025, leading to it entering the [US public
domain](2025_in_public_domain "wikilink").

## Home media

The short was released on December 4, 2001, on *[Walt Disney Treasures:
Silly Symphonies - The Historic Musical Animated
Classics](Walt_Disney_Treasures:_Wave_One#Silly_Symphonies "wikilink")*[^14][^15]
and on December 2, 2002, on *[Walt Disney Treasures: Mickey Mouse in
Black and
White](Walt_Disney_Treasures:_Wave_Two#Mickey_Mouse_in_Black_and_White "wikilink")*.[^16]
It was included as a bonus feature on the Diamond Edition Blu-ray of
2009 of *[Snow White and the Seven
Dwarfs](Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_(1937_film) "wikilink")*. It was
released to [Disney+](Disney+ "wikilink") on July 7, 2023.[^17]

## Legacy

[Ub Iwerks](Ub_Iwerks "wikilink") left Disney in 1929 to found [his own
studio](Animated_Pictures "wikilink") with [Pat
Powers](Pat_Powers_(producer) "wikilink"), whose cartoons were
unsuccessful, leading to Iwerks subcontracting work to his former
employer [Screen Gems](Screen_Gems_(animation_studio) "wikilink"),
[Charles Mintz](Charles_Mintz "wikilink")'s studio which was owned by
Columbia Pictures; he directed the *[Color
Rhapsody](Color_Rhapsody "wikilink")* cartoon *Skeleton Frolic*, a
spiritual successor to *The Skeleton Dance* produced in
[Technicolor](Technicolor "wikilink"), which was released in 1937.[^18]

*The Skeleton Dance* appears in the 2012 video game *[Epic Mickey 2: The
Power of Two](Epic_Mickey_2:_The_Power_of_Two "wikilink")* as an
unlockable short.[^19] The film was parodied in *[The Grim Adventures of
Billy & Mandy](The_Grim_Adventures_of_Billy_&_Mandy "wikilink")* episode
"Hill Billy", where Grim recalls dancing with skeletons 300 years
prior.[^20] *The Skeleton Dance* experienced a resurgence in popularity
in the late 2000s and early 2010s, largely due to its pairing with the
[Andrew Gold](Andrew_Gold "wikilink") song "[Spooky, Scary
Skeletons](Spooky,_Scary_Skeletons "wikilink")" as a recurring meme on
platforms like, [Reddit](Reddit "wikilink") and
[YouTube](YouTube "wikilink").[^21]
